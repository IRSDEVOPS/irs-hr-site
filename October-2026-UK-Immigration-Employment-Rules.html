What's Changing in October 2026?

The Home Office is tightening regulations around recruitment and employment networks. Here is exactly what Sponsor Licence holders need to know.

Extended Liability for Right to Work

Effective: 1 October 2026

Historically, liability for illegal working fines (civil penalties) rested solely with the direct employer. The new rules extend this responsibility. In certain contractual arrangements, liability for Right to Work checks may extend beyond the direct employer to prime contractors or overarching clients.

  • Fines: Civil penalties can be applied to the primary business if a subcontractor uses illegal labor.
  • Sponsor Licence Risk: Breaches within your recruitment and employment networks could trigger Home Office audits and potential suspension of your own Sponsor Licence.

Contractual Obligations

You must implement robust indemnities and compliance clauses in all Service Level Agreements (SLAs) with agencies and subcontractors.

Digital eVisa Transition Finalization

While initiated earlier, October marks strict enforcement of the fully digital immigration system. Physical Biometric Residence Permits (BRPs) are entirely phased out. All RTW checks must be done via share codes.

Sponsor Duties Enhancement

Reporting requirements for sponsored workers on the SMS (Sponsorship Management System) are becoming stricter, with reduced timeframes for reporting changes in work locations or hybrid working patterns.

Right to Work Fines & Enforcement

The Home Office has drastically increased civil penalties for non-compliance. Under the extended liability rules coming in October 2026, you can be held liable for workers supplied by third parties if proper checks weren't conducted.

  • First Offense: Up to £45,000 per illegal worker.
  • Repeat Offense: Up to £60,000 per illegal worker (within 3 years).
  • Criminal Sanctions: Up to 5 years imprisonment and unlimited fines for serious breaches.
  • Licence Revocation: Immediate risk to your Sponsor Licence, which can freeze your ability to hire or retain overseas talent.

Your 4-Step Action Plan

01

Map Your Recruitment and Employment

Identify all third-party suppliers, recruitment agencies, and subcontractors providing labor to your sites or projects.

02

Review Contracts

Update terms of business to include strict RTW compliance warranties and indemnities against civil penalties.

03

Audit Suppliers

Establish a mechanism to conduct regular, random audits of your subcontractors' onboarding and RTW check procedures.

04

Digitize Records

Move away from paper records. Ensure all statutory excuses are stored securely online with expiration alerts.

Recruitment and Employment RTW Risk Assessor

Answer 3 quick questions to instantly evaluate your exposure to the October 2026 extended liability rules.

1. Does your business utilize contractors, agency workers, or outsourced labor?

2. Do your current supplier contracts include specific indemnities holding the supplier financially liable if they provide an illegal worker?

3. Do you have a system in place to proactively audit your suppliers' Right to Work checks on at least an annual basis?

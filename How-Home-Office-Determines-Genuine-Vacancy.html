How the Home Office Determines a
"Genuine Vacancy"

Failing the genuine vacancy test is a leading cause of sponsor licence revocations. Understand the rules, assess your roles, and protect your business from Home Office enforcement.

The Definitive Guide to the Genuine Vacancy Test

Under the UK's immigration sponsorship regime, the ability to sponsor migrant workers is a conditional privilege. To protect the integrity of the border, the Home Office applies the rigorous "Genuine Vacancy" test before and during sponsorship.

Paragraph C1.44: The Objective Definition

According to Home Office guidance, a vacancy is only considered "genuine" if it objectively meets three non-negotiable criteria:

  • Duties match the SOC code: The jobholder must actually perform the specific duties and responsibilities matching the assigned Standard Occupational Classification (SOC) code.
  • No lower-skilled duties: The role must not predominantly include lower-skilled administrative or manual tasks that fall below the skill threshold for the visa route.
  • Business appropriateness: The role must make logical and commercial sense for the business given its current scale, business plan, and operating model.

Red Flags: What Triggers a Revocation? (Paragraph C1.46)

The Home Office proactively looks for indicators of abuse or manipulation. If an enforcement officer suspects any of the following during an audit, they will likely infer that the role was created to facilitate a visa, resulting in immediate licence suspension or revocation:

Exaggerated Job Descriptions

Assigning a "Manager" SOC code to someone who primarily works the shop floor, stocks shelves, or performs entry-level tasks.

Fictitious Roles

Roles that do not actually exist in reality, created solely on paper to justify issuing a Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS).

Illogical Hires

Hiring someone for a role they objectively cannot perform (e.g., hiring a non-driver for a domiciliary care role that requires driving between clients). This is treated as a systemic failure in recruitment and a threat to immigration control.

Action Plan: What Employers Must Do

  • Conduct Granular Role Audits Regularly observe sponsored workers. Ensure their day-to-day reality exactly matches the duties listed on their CoS. If they have evolved into a different role, you must update the Home Office.
  • Retain Exhaustive Recruitment Records Keep documented proof of why the role was needed, interview notes, and evidence that the candidate meets all mandatory requirements (e.g., English language tests, driving licences).
  • Implement Task Logging Require workers to maintain a contemporaneous log of their activities to serve as a defensive mechanism against allegations of title inflation during an unannounced compliance visit.

Buying or selling a business? What happens to the sponsor licence, and the sponsored workers?

Immigration compliance rarely tops your M&A checklist, but a direct change of ownership has immediate, severe consequences for your Skilled Worker visa holders. Remember: Sponsor licences are not transferable.

Use Your 20-Day Compliance Tool Read Your Obligations

The Core Rule: Your Licence Cannot Be Transferred

If your legal entity changes through an asset sale, TUPE transfer, or a restructure, the new business owner (the buyer) must hold their own sponsor licence or apply for one immediately.

Your 20-Working-Day Deadline

Your sponsor licence reporting duties do not pause while you handle a corporate transaction. You must notify the Home Office (UKVI) of relevant ownership changes within a strict 20 working day timeframe from the date of completion.

Missing this deadline triggers immediate Home Office compliance action against your licence. Furthermore, UKVI reviews Companies House records during their compliance checks. If you fail to report historic transactions, they can come back to haunt you months or years later.

Pro Tip: Immigration due diligence is essential *before* you acquire a business that employs sponsored workers.

Share Sales

A change in direct ownership usually renders the existing licence invalid. If you are buying, you must submit a new application within 20 days.

TUPE Transfers

As the new employer, you assume responsibility for transferred workers, provided you hold a valid licence and report it on your SMS.

Right to Work

As a buyer, you have a 60-day grace period post-transfer to conduct fresh Right to Work checks on all your newly acquired TUPE employees.

Historic Audits

UKVI cross-references with Companies House. Your historic, unreported M&A activity can lead to sudden, unexpected licence revocations.

The Cost of Getting Your Compliance Wrong

Gaps in Sponsorship

If you aren't licensed in time, your employees will suffer a critical gap in their sponsorship, rendering their immigration status unlawful.

New Visas Required

Depending on how you structure the deal, you may need to issue new Certificates of Sponsorship (CoS) and pay for fresh visa applications.

Employee Uncertainty

You risk causing high anxiety and uncertainty for your employees regarding their right to live and work in the UK.

Licence Revocation

You face potential licence suspension or revocation—affecting not just your transferred employees, but your entire sponsored workforce.

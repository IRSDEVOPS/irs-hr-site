Urgent Compliance Update based on High Court Rulings

How Unreported Unpaid Leave
Triggers Licence Revocation

UKVI is cross-referencing HMRC payroll data. A single missed 10-day SMS report for an employee's unpaid leave can now result in automatic sponsor licence suspension or revocation.

Check Your Compliance Risk Book a Free Compliance Audit

The New Era of Algorithmic Enforcement

Administrative oversights are no longer just warnings. The Home Office treats a failure to report unpaid leave as a mandatory breach of your sponsor duties.

HMRC RTI Data Matching

Under the Data Use and Access Act (DUAA) 2025, UKVI algorithms continuously cross-reference your SMS data with HMRC Real Time Information. If a worker takes unpaid leave, their pay drops below the CoS threshold. The algorithm flags this instantly.

The "Moon Fish" Precedent

Recent High Court battles demonstrate UKVI's aggressive stance. Even lawful, compassionate unpaid leave will trigger a mandatory revocation ground (Annex C1) if you fail to report it via the SMS within the strict 10-day window.

Devastating Consequences

Licence revocation invalidates all existing Certificates of Sponsorship (CoS). Your sponsored workers' visas will be curtailed to 60 days, fundamentally disrupting your operations and exposing you to reputational damage.

The Two Rules You Must Not Confuse

HR departments frequently confuse the substantive limit on unpaid leave with the procedural requirement to report it. Failing on either front is catastrophic.

1

The 4-Week Limit (Substantive Rule)

A sponsored worker cannot take more than 4 weeks of unpaid leave in a single calendar year (Jan 1 - Dec 31). If they exceed this without an approved statutory exception (like maternity or sick leave), you must terminate their sponsorship immediately.

2

The 10-Day Report (Procedural Rule)

Regardless of how long the leave is, or if it falls under an exception, you must report the reduction in salary/absence on the SMS within 10 working days. This is where most employers fail, triggering automated audits.

Permitted Exceptions

Even if an exception applies to the 4-week rule, the 10-day reporting rule is ALWAYS active.

  • Statutory Maternity/Paternity Leave
  • Statutory Sick Leave
  • Assisting in a national/international crisis
  • Legal industrial action
  • Compassionate Leave: Highly scrutinized. Permitted only if evidence is provided and formally accepted by UKVI via an SMS report.

What Employers Must Do Now

Protect your licence by implementing these critical HR and operational safeguards immediately.

1

Audit Payroll Data

Reconcile current pay data with CoS stated salaries before HMRC RTI submissions are finalized.

2

Bridge HR & Payroll

Ensure any period of unpaid leave logged by HR triggers an immediate alert to the SMS compliance officer.

3

Strict 10-Day SLA

Implement an internal 5-day Service Level Agreement to ensure all SMS reports are filed well before the 10-day legal limit.

4

Maintain Evidence

Keep an unalterable audit trail for every absence: Request, Approval, Reason, and confirmation of SMS reporting.

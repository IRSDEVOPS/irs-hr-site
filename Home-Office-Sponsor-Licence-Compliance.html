What you need to know

Practical Action

The Routine That Will
Save Your Licence in 2026

Compliance is no longer a task you can revisit haphazardly. It’s a cross-functional governance risk. To avoid sudden audits, implement this essential rhythm across HR, payroll, and line management.

Weekly: The 'Changes' Sync

HR, Line Managers, and Immigration must communicate before changes happen.

  • Are any sponsored workers changing roles or duties?
  • Has anyone requested a change in working hours?

Monthly: CoS Reconciliation

The single most important check to prevent payroll-triggered compliance audits.

  • Export current payroll data for all sponsored workers.
  • Compare actual pay directly against their active CoS.
  • Report any permitted discrepancies on the SMS instantly.

Ongoing: Digital Readiness

Prepare for short-notice, remote Home Office information requests.

  • Maintain clear records of digital share codes (eVisas).
  • Track "New Entrant" rate expiry dates meticulously.

Q3 & Q4 Trends

4 Critical Risk Areas

Avoid licence suspension, brutal civil penalties, and workforce disruption by addressing these top risk factors right now.

1. eVisas & Student Limits

The civil penalty regime expands October 1, 2026. With eVisas taking over, clear digital share code tracking is non-negotiable. Don't assume term dates are obvious.

  • Enforce strict student term limits
  • Verify status before summer transitions

2. Zero Tolerance for Errors

"Innocent" administrative errors now trigger severe action if they reveal weak internal controls. Breaches don't need to be deliberate to ruin your licence.

  • Prepare for sudden remote checks
  • Document *how* you fixed system flaws

3. The Payroll Disconnect

The #1 current risk. Payroll increases a salary or changes hours, but HR forgets to update the SMS. The Home Office spots this data mismatch instantly.

  • Force communication between HR & Payroll
  • Audit SMS vs Payroll monthly

4. Scrutiny on Job Roles

"Eligible roles" have replaced genuine vacancies. Borderline occupation codes are heavily scrutinized. If duties evolve, your CoS might be invalid.

  • Re-evaluate roles as duties change
  • Never let a New Entrant rate expire

Urgent Update: September 2026

The Home Office introduces The ulti-Factor Authentication in September 2026

Major updates to the SMS are rolling out. From mandatory MFA to the removal of Level 2 users, ensure your organization remains compliant to protect your sponsor licence.

Get Compliance Help Read the Changes

4 Critical Changes to Your Sponsor Licence

The Home Office has published updated guidance for Sponsors. Failure to comply with these system changes could result in the loss of your licence.

1. Mandatory MFA (Sept 3, 2026)

Multi-Factor Authentication will be required for all SMS users via a One-Time Passcode (OTP) sent to mobile or email. New licences from Sept 9 will have this enabled automatically.

  • Worker Licences: Phased from Sept 3, 2026
  • Student Licences: Early Nov 2026

2. Removal of Level 2 Users

From 9 September 2026, you can no longer appoint Level 2 users. You must upgrade existing eligible Level 2 users to Level 1, or deactivate their accounts by 8 March 2027 to maintain their system access.

3. Deactivation of Inactive Accounts

The Home Office will actively remove inactive SMS user accounts from your licence. Warning: You risk losing your sponsor licence entirely if you do not take action to check and update your SMS user details regularly.

4. New Activity Notifications

Starting Sept 9, Authorising Officers will receive email alerts for key activities: changing L1 user details, adding new L1 users, or changing the AO email. Sponsors under 1,000 CoS/CAS will also be notified of individual/batch assignments.

Very Important: Avoiding Cyber Scams

The Home Office has issued a strict warning regarding phishing attempts targeting SMS users during this transition.

The Home Office will NEVER:

  • × Send you a link directly to the SMS login page or SMS guidance.
  • × Send attachments with embedded links to the SMS login page.
  • × Send links to view messages or take action outside the SMS system.
  • × Ask for your SMS user ID, password, OTP, or DOB outside the system.
  • × Send you an OTP when you have not explicitly requested one.

The Home Office will ALWAYS:

  • ✓ Advise you to manually navigate to www.gov.uk and search for the SMS login.
  • ✓ Advise you to securely check/update personal details within the SMS yourself.
  • ✓ Warn you not to click on links regarding SMS sent via email.
Compromised? If you suspect your account is compromised, immediately contact BusinessHelpdesk@homeoffice.gov.uk.

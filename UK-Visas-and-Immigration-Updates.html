Statement of Changes HC 584

Find out everything about Statement of changes HC 584

The latest UK Immigration Rules update introduces major protections for modern slavery victims, prepares for the Erasmus+ scheme, and tightens compliance regulations.

Detailed Rule Changes

Expand each section below to read the specific details regarding the immigration rule changes.

Historically, the Skilled Worker visa strictly tied an employee to a single sponsor, making it difficult for victims of exploitation to report abuse without risking their immigration status.

The Change (SW 18.3): If a sponsored worker is referred to the National Referral Mechanism (NRM) and receives a positive Conclusive Grounds decision, their visa conditions are altered.

  • Unrestricted Employment: Victims are granted open-market work rights for the remainder of their visa validity.
  • Flexibility: They can work for any employer, take up self-employment, or undertake voluntary work (excluding professional sports roles).

Previously, the Victim of Domestic Abuse settlement route was mostly limited to dependent partners and minor children of British citizens, settled persons, or refugees.

The Change: HC 584 expands this route (Appendix VDA) to include adult dependent children (aged 18 or over). If an adult dependent child is in the UK and their relationship with their parent or parent's partner permanently breaks down due to domestic abuse, they can now apply for settlement in their own right, preventing them from being trapped in abusive households.

Following agreements to associate with the Erasmus+ scheme from 2027, the Visitor and Student routes have been expanded:

  • Visitor Route: Visitors can undertake Erasmus+ activities, including traineeships (up to 30 days), job shadowing, "participative observation," and coaching assignments.
  • Student Routes: Erasmus+ participants are exempt from the standard requirement that a course must lead to an approved qualification. They can study at state-funded schools/academies (if the institution holds a sponsor licence) and are exempt from standard work placement duration limits.
  • Absconding and Bail: Part Suitability is amended so applicants in the UK can be refused permission to stay if they failed to report under bail conditions or absconded from custody.
  • Biometrics: Varying an existing application will not reset the biometric enrollment deadline.
  • Fee Waivers: If an applicant requests a fee waiver for a human rights claim but subsequently submits an application on a different route, the application date is the date of submission, not the fee waiver request date (risking Section 3C leave).
  • Retaining Status: Provisions added to allow some pre-settled status holders who no longer meet strict EUSS eligibility to remain in the scheme if stripping status would be disproportionate.
  • Derivative Rights: Clarifications made regarding continuous qualifying periods for primary carers.
  • Proof of Identity (From 9 Dec 2026): Applicants can no longer use an expired Biometric Residence Permit (BRP) to prove identity and nationality for the EUSS.

A progressive change for families on the Hong Kong BN(O) route. A dependent child (under 18) will no longer need to complete their own separate 5-year continuous residence period. As long as their most recent permission was on the BN(O) route, they can settle alongside a parent who meets the settlement requirements.

  • Student Maintenance (30 Nov 2026): Financial requirements increase from £1,529 to £1,570/month for London, and from £1,171 to £1,203/month outside London.
  • Religious Workers: Must be paid the National Minimum Wage. If legally exempt, the A-rated sponsor must formally guarantee maintenance and accommodation.
  • Family Returns Process: Families can now raise human rights/Article 8 claims directly with Home Office staff managing their return without needing a formal application or fee.

Employer Briefing for UK Sponsor Licence Holders

What is the Home Office's 1/12th Salary Rule, And, how you might be breaching your sponsor duties?

In Treal Care [2026] EWCA Civ 1420, the Court of Appeal rejected the Home Office’s rigid claim that sponsors must pay at least 1/12th of an annual CoS salary every single month. Yet under the April 2026 SW 14.3B rules, severe shortfalls over a consecutive three-month period will still trigger licence revocation.

Key Legal Questions Answered

What Sponsor Licence Holders Need to Know Right Now

Clear, authoritative answers to the two critical compliance questions shaping UKVI enforcement after November 2026.

Home Office Argument vs. Court Ruling [2026] EWCA Civ 1420

“The Secretary of State advanced the argument that, for monthly-paid workers whose CoS states an annual salary figure, the sponsor assumes an absolute obligation to pay at least one-twelfth of that annual amount in every single month.”

Direct Answer: Rejected by the Court of Appeal

In R (Treal Care (UK) Limited) v SSHD [2026] EWCA Civ 1420, Lord Justice Peter Jackson (with Green and Fancourt LJJ agreeing) ruled that this rigid 1/12th monthly requirement is wrong in principle and inconsistent with the Sponsor Guidance.

Where a sponsored worker works uneven or fluctuating shift patterns, a single month dipping below 1/12th of the annual Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS) figure does not automatically breach Annex C1(aa). Instead, UKVI must conduct a rounded, evidence-based assessment over a fair sample period (such as 4 months) and consider valid explanations—including authorised unpaid leave, shift swaps across pay periods, or payroll reconciliation lump sums.

Key Takeaway: One low month ≠ automatic revocation. Test Monthly Variance →
Mandatory Rule Change Appendix Skilled Worker SW 14.3B

“Sponsors can no longer rely purely on annualised figures if their payroll practices cause severe shortfalls over a consecutive three-month period.”

Direct Answer: Enforced via the 1/4 Quarterly Floor

While Treal Care defeats a mechanical 1-month trigger, the April 2026 Immigration Rules introduced SW 14.3B, creating a strict statutory floor over any rolling 3-month window (or 12-week window for weekly-paid staff).

Employers cannot underpay a worker for an entire quarter and promise to "make it up at the end of the year." For monthly-paid workers with uneven hours, gross pay across every rolling three-month period must equal at least one-quarter (25%) of the stated annual CoS salary—unless the shortfall is caused by a permitted statutory absence (such as statutory maternity, paternity, or certified sick leave).

Key Takeaway: Rolling 3-month pay must be ≥ 25% of annual CoS. Run 3-Month Audit →
Inside the Judgment

Why the Home Office Lost in Treal Care

Treal Care (UK) Ltd sponsored 75 care workers on CoS salaries of £22,400 per year (based on a 40-hour week). Following a March 2024 compliance visit, the Home Office revoked their licence under Annex C1(aa) (underpayment) and Annex C1(z) (genuine vacancy) after reviewing four months of payslips (May–August 2024) for three workers:

Worker 1: Ms A Revocation Irrational

Annualised pay over the 4 months was £22,190 (marginally below £22,400) only because she took authorised unpaid leave in May and July. Her June and August pay met or exceeded the CoS rate. UKVI ignored the unpaid leave explanation.

Worker 2: Ms O Revocation Irrational

Annualised pay over the 4-month period was £22,693.56—exceeding her £22,400 CoS! An earlier administrative lag in notifying training hours to payroll had already been rectified by a lump-sum payment before the revocation decision.

Worker 3: Ms L Residual Discretion Applies

Worked reduced hours while caring for her seriously ill son (compassionate leave) and was later topped up. The Court confirmed that the Secretary of State holds a residual discretion even under "mandatory" Annex C1 grounds to consider mitigation and proportionality.

The New 2026 Dual-Layer Salary Framework

How HR and Payroll teams must reconcile the Treal Care Court of Appeal ruling with the April 2026 Skilled Worker Rules (`SW 14.3A` and `SW 14.3B`).

Single-Month Shortfall Paying < 1/12th of annual CoS in any single month = automatic breach. Permitted if justified: Requires a rounded assessment across the working pattern & evidence.
Rolling 3-Month Floor (SW 14.3B) No explicit quarterly rule; arbitrary monthly spreadsheet checks. Strict 25% Minimum: Must pay ≥ 1/4 of annual CoS salary in every consecutive 3-month period.

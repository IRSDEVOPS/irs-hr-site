Questions Sponsor Licence Holders Ask Google & AI Chatbots About Pre-Revocation Letters

Direct, authoritative answers for Authorising Officers, Care Directors, and HR Managers navigating UKVI compliance checks, Annex C1(aa), and CoS allocations in 2026.

A letter from SponsorAssuranceAdmin@homeoffice.gov.uk (the Home Office Sponsor Assurance / Revocation Unit) is a formal compliance enforcement investigation. Between October 2025 and June 2026, over 8,391 sponsor licences were suspended or revoked following these investigations.

The letter typically cites Annex C1(aa) of the Workers and Temporary Workers Sponsor Guidance—which is a mandatory revocation ground if the Home Office believes you paid a sponsored worker less than stated on their Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS) without proper reporting or permitted exemptions. You are given strictly 20 working days to submit comprehensive documentary evidence (under 25MB per email, no cloud links or passwords allowed).

This is the #1 blind spot for UK employers. Even if a worker's total annual pay exceeds their CoS figure, the Home Office's automated HMRC Real Time Information (RTI) data matching flags sponsors for three common reasons:

  • The 3-Month Quarter Rule & 17-Week Irregular Pattern Rule: For regular weekly hours, pay over any 3-month period must equal at least 1/4 of the required annual salary. For uneven shift patterns, you must have confirmed the irregular pattern on the CoS/SMS, and pay over any 17-week period must equal at least 17/52 of the annual salary.
  • Unlogged Unpaid Absences: If a worker takes unpaid sick leave or parental/emergency leave, their monthly RTI pay drops. Unless you can produce system-timestamped screenshots of when the absence was originally logged on your HR system (plus fit notes), UKVI treats the dip as an unlawful salary reduction.
  • Deductions & Bulk Payroll Discrepancies: Unexplained deductions or bulk payroll transfers that do not clearly reconcile to individual payslips and corporate bank statements trigger immediate scrutiny.

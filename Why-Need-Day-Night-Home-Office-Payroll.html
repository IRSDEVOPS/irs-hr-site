Why You Need Day and Night Home Office Payroll & CoS Compliance

Putting a compliant annual salary on a Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS) is only step one. Since 8 April 2026 (Paragraph SW 14.3B), sponsors must prove compliance across every single pay cycle:

Every Hour Worked Pay must meet the occupation's going rate for every actual hour worked in each pay period.
Rolling Windows Earnings are tested across rolling 3-month, 12-week, or 17-week spans.
HMRC RTI Checks Home Office teams cross-check your real PAYE filings against CoS declarations.
Quick Answer: What Is "Day & Night" Compliance?
  • Routine, Every-Run Monitoring: Checking sponsored pay on every payroll run—not just before a Home Office audit.
  • No Hidden Dips: Because HMRC shares PAYE RTI data, an unmonitored drop in hours or an excluded perk can flag a discrepancy immediately.
  • Prevention Over Back-Pay: Paying arrears later fixes payroll records, but it does not automatically erase an immigration rule breach.

SW 14.3B Rolling Threshold Matrix

1

Every Single Pay Period (Hourly Test)

Earnings must equal at least the applicable going rate for every actual hour worked in each individual pay cycle.

2A

Monthly or Less Frequent Pay

Rolling 3-month window: Qualifying pay must equal at least ¼ (25%) of the required yearly salary.

2B

More Frequent Than Monthly (e.g. Weekly)

Rolling 12-week window: Qualifying pay must equal at least 12/52 of the required yearly salary.

2C

Irregular Working Patterns

Rolling 17-week window: Where weekly hours fluctuate causing uneven pay, earnings must hit at least 17/52 of the required yearly salary (plus sponsor confirmation of the work pattern).

The 8 April 2026 Shift

Why Pay-Period Compliance Matters for Sponsors

The Old Assumption

"The annual contract salary matches the CoS."

Looking only at the yearly headline figure in an employment contract or on a Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS) is no longer enough.

Since 8 April 2026 (SW 14.3B)

How pay is delivered over time is now tested.

Under paragraph SW 14.3B of Appendix Skilled Worker, actual pay must meet strict rolling windows and hourly going rates in every pay period.

Who Needs Extra Vigilance?

Variable Pay Staff

Sponsored employees whose take-home pay fluctuates from one cycle to the next.

Irregular Shift Workers

Staff working non-standard patterns or uneven weekly hours.

Changing Roles/Hours

Workers experiencing adjustments to their working setup or contractual terms.

Golden Rule for Payroll & HR Teams:

  • Never treat a CoS salary figure in isolation.
  • Check that real pay and actual hours match Home Office records on every payroll run.
  • Make compliance part of routine payroll—not a last-minute scramble before an audit.
02
Appendix Skilled Worker

Paragraph SW 14.3B: The 3 Core Pay Rules

Paragraph SW 14.3B sets out how sponsored staff must be paid and how their earnings are tested over time. You must satisfy both the hourly rule and the applicable rolling window:

Rule 1: Every Pay Period

Meet the Hourly Going Rate

  • Pay must meet the going rate for every actual hour worked in each period.
  • You must check real hours worked and real pay—not just the yearly contract number.
Rule 2: Rolling Windows

Pass the Rolling Minimum Test

  • Assessed on a rolling basis—not just static calendar months or quarters.
  • Averaging over a window never waives Rule 1 (the hourly going rate).

Which Rolling Window Applies to Your Worker?

Monthly (or Less Frequent)

3-Month Window

Must receive at least ¼ (25%) of the required annual salary over any rolling 3 months.

Formula: Annual Req ÷ 4
More Frequent (e.g. Weekly)

12-Week Window

Must receive at least 12/52 of the required annual salary over any rolling 12 weeks.

Formula: Annual Req × (12 / 52)
Irregular Working Pattern

17-Week Window

Where weekly hours vary causing uneven pay: must hit 17/52 over any 17 weeks plus sponsor confirmation of the pattern.

Formula: Annual Req × (17 / 52)

CoS Salary Calculation

Which Salary Elements Actually Count?

Key Principle: The salary figure entered on a CoS must only reflect remuneration recognised by the Immigration Rules—never automatically an employee's total reward package.

1. Payments to Evaluate Carefully

Starting Point

Basic Gross Salary

  • Forms the baseline of your calculation.
  • Must be checked against both the general threshold and occupation going rate for the worker's salary option.
Restricted

Allowances

  • Rules strictly limit which allowances qualify.
  • Never assume London weighting or contractual supplements can automatically be added.
High Caution

Bonuses & Variable Pay

  • Be cautious with overtime, commission, or bonuses.
  • Discretionary or occasional payouts must not be treated as guaranteed pay.

2. Six Payments You Should NOT Automatically Count

Reimbursed expenses paid by the employer
Benefits in kind (e.g. medical cover, company car)
Employer pension contributions
One-off sign-on payments
Non-qualifying discretionary bonuses & overtime
Unverified commission or variable pay

3. Check Deductions & Money Paid Back to the Employer

Salary Deductions: Can lower the pay figure that counts for immigration rules.
Loan Repayments: Repayments to the sponsor can reduce qualifying salary.
Business Investments: Money invested into the sponsor reduces recognised pay.

Safe Rule: If any payment or deduction is unclear, resolve how it is treated before relying on it to meet the CoS threshold.

04
Real-Time Verification

HMRC PAYE / RTI Data Matching & Audits

Step 1

PAYE & RTI Submissions

You report employee earnings to HMRC on every pay run via Real Time Information (RTI).

Step 2

Home Office Cross-Checks

Caseworkers inspect HMRC payroll data to verify whether sponsored staff are paid as declared.

Step 3

Three-Way Alignment

Your HR files, payroll outputs, and SMS sponsorship records must match.

Example: The £45,000 CoS Mismatch

A worker's CoS states £45,000/yr, but HMRC payroll records show far lower pay. Even if a valid reason exists, you must understand the variance and hold proof that the worker still meets the rules.

6 Red Flags That Trigger Compliance Questions

1. Pay Below Threshold

Actual earnings dropping below the required floor.

2. CoS Mismatches

Payroll records differing from CoS pay or hours.

3. Late / Missing RTI

Overdue or omitted payroll submissions to HMRC.

4. Unexplained Shifts

Unaccounted-for changes in hours or pay rates.

5. Extended Reduced Pay

Long periods of unpaid or reduced-pay leave.

6. Qualifying Deductions

Deductions that lower immigration-recognised pay.

Good to Know: A payroll difference doesn't automatically prove a breach or trigger an investigation—but unexplained inconsistencies cause avoidable trouble. Submit RTI on time and investigate gaps immediately.
05
Enforcement & Visa Impact

What Happens if a Sponsored Worker Is Underpaid?

Risks to the Sponsoring Employer

  • Rating Downgrade: Lowering your sponsor licence rating where applicable.
  • Licence Suspension: Freezing your licence while concerns are investigated.
  • Licence Revocation: Losing your licence and ability to sponsor workers on the route.
  • Hiring Restrictions: Limiting your ability to sponsor additional overseas staff.

Impact on the Sponsored Employee

  • Permission Risk: If sponsorship ends or rules aren't met, the Home Office may act on their visa permission.
  • Case-by-Case Outcome: Visa curtailment is not automatic in every situation—it depends on the specific facts and applicable rules.

Why Paying Arrears Later Doesn't Automatically Erase a Breach

Making a retrospective top-up fixes your payroll records, but does not automatically cure an Immigration Rules breach. If you spot a shortfall, take these 4 steps immediately:

1. Investigate the root cause of the shortfall.
2. Calculate the exact deficit amount.
3. Correct any ongoing payroll error.
4. Get Advice on the immigration impact.
06
Practical Employer Checklist

What To Do: 5-Step Sponsor Action Plan

Step 1

Connect Payroll with CoS Records

  • Share CoS salary, salary option, going rate, and expected hours with payroll.
  • Flag sponsored staff clearly so payroll knows pay changes carry visa consequences.
Step 2

Test the Right Rolling Window

  • Check the 3-month, 12-week, or 17-week window plus the hourly going rate every period.
  • Investigate any fluctuating hours or pay to ensure the setup is permitted.
Step 3

Keep Unified Audit Evidence

  • Store contracts, CoS files, payslips, payroll runs, and hour logs together.
  • Document clear explanations and proof whenever actual pay differs from expectations.
Step 4

Run Monthly & Quarterly Audits

  • Combine a monthly salary review with quarterly internal compliance audits.
  • Train payroll staff on Skilled Worker pay rules so issues are caught early.

Step 5: Escalate Changes Fast (Check the 10-Working-Day SMS Rule)

SMS Reporting Duty

HR, payroll, and your sponsor licence manager must communicate immediately whenever any of these 6 events occur:

Salary increases or drops
Contractual hour changes
Unpaid leave / long absences
Working pattern shifts
Salary deductions
Revised employment terms

*Certain updates must be reported via the Sponsorship Management System (SMS)—generally within 10 working days where the reporting duty applies. Always check the exact reporting deadline for each specific change.
